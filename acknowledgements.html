Acknowledgements

Other people’s ideas, cut down to a pocket.

Next Page invents very little. It takes a handful of ideas from systems that already work and drops the parts that need upkeep. This page says where each idea came from, what was left behind, and which popular systems it doesn’t use at all.

Compared

Sixteen systems and ideas, side by side

SystemThe ideaWhat Next Page takesWhat it leaves out
Do It TomorrowMark Forster, 2006Borrowed A closed “will do” list for each day. Anything new waits until tomorrow. The core rule: today’s list is closed and new things go on tomorrow’s page. Also the pressure on items that keep moving.Rule 2 · Slip cards 04, 05, 06, 10 The task diary layout and the formal backlog-clearing project.
Getting Things DoneDavid Allen, 2001Borrowed Capture every open loop, turn each into a next action, sort into lists, review weekly. Next physical action, the Waiting for and Someday pages, the calendar for hard dates, the weekly look back, and the two-minute rule in the evening sort.Rule 3 · Slip cards 03, 15–18, 22 Contexts, capturing every loop, project outcome lists and the full weekly review checklist.
Bullet JournalRyder Carroll, 2013Borrowed Rapid logging with short bullets; migrate unfinished items by hand so each one is a decision. The marks (• × › –) and migration as a decision: rewriting is the filter.Slip cards 04, 14, 21 The index, future log, monthly log, collections and decorated spreads.
The Now HabitNeil Fiore, 1989Borrowed Beat procrastination with short starts, guilt-free stopping and play scheduled first. Thirty-minute starts on the focus item, permission to stop, and starting small after a gap.Slip cards 02, 08, 09, 11, 12, 23 The Unschedule (planning leisure first) and the reverse calendar.
D-A-F-TAndrea CannavinaBorrowed Four verbs for clearing paper and email: Defer, Act, File, Toss. The two-minute evening sort for leftover items, as an add-on.Full card · fold-up guide p4 Its use as an inbox and paperwork method.
Habit stackingBJ Fogg, Tiny Habits (2019); James Clear, Atomic Habits (2018)Borrowed Anchor a new habit to one you already have. Clear adds “never miss twice”. The Hook rule for every add-on: it rides on something you already do. Also hanging the Sunday flip on an existing routine, and restarting after a gap.Slip cards 11, 22 Habit design for anything beyond the notebook itself.
The planning fallacyKahneman & Tversky, 1979Borrowed Research, not a system: people reliably underestimate how long their own tasks take. The capacity check: plan the hours you have, not the day you want.Slip card 01 Formal estimation and tracking.
Ivy Lee methodIvy Lee, about 1918Shared Each evening, write tomorrow’s six most important tasks in order. Work down them. Carry the rest. Nothing taken directly, but the core is close to it. A short list, written ahead, with leftovers carried forward, has been around for a century. The fixed six and the strict order.
Personal KanbanJim Benson & Tonianne DeMaria Barry, 2011Shared Two rules: make your work visible, and limit work in progress. A work-in-progress limit: no more than five projects on the Now page.Slip card 17 The board, columns and moving cards.
Pomodoro TechniqueFrancesco Cirillo, late 1980sShared Work in 25-minute timed blocks with short breaks, and count them. A fixed block to start the focus item (thirty minutes, from Fiore). The timer, the counting and the break schedule.
Time blockingPopularised by Cal Newport, Deep Work (2016)Shared Give every hour of the day a job in advance. Only the capacity check: count your free hours before you write the list.Slip card 01 The hour-by-hour schedule and re-planning it when the day changes.
Eisenhower matrixPopularised by Stephen Covey, 1989Not used Sort every task into urgent/important quadrants. One question in its place: which single item would make today count?Slip card 02 Sorting every item into four boxes, which is upkeep.
Trello and Kanban appsTrello launched 2011Not used Digital boards of cards moved across columns. Nothing. The notebook’s only columns are today and tomorrow. A screen to open and a board to keep tidy. Fine alongside Next Page for shared team work.
Habit trackersCommon in Bullet Journal practiceNot used A grid of habits ticked off every day. Not in the core. The One habit add-on tracks a single habit on the Weeks page, and stops once it sticks. Another daily grid to fill in, and the guilt of a broken streak.
The 5 Second RuleMel Robbins, 2017Not used Count 5-4-3-2-1 and move before hesitation wins. Nothing directly. It works well with slip card 23 if you need a trigger to start the top item.Pairs with slip card 23 Nothing to leave: it’s a starting trick, not a list system.
AutofocusMark Forster, 2009Not used One long open list; scan it and work on whatever stands out. Nothing. Next Page closes the list that Autofocus keeps open. The long list and the scanning.
