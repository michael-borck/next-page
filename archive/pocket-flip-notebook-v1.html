Run your day from a pocket flip notebook

A set of six symbols, one page per day and a two-minute evening sort. It works with a $1 top-spiral notebook or one you staple together yourself.

Set up a new notebook in three minutes

You need a notebook and a pen that does not bleed through. Nothing else.

  1. Label the cover. Add a phone number or email and the start date. A pocket notebook gets lost.
  2. Leave the first page for an index. As you fill pages worth finding again, add a line such as “p.12 Kitchen ideas”. Number only those pages.
  3. Start day pages straight after the index. Write the date at the top of each new day.
  4. Flip the notebook over and start the back. Turn it upside down and give the first back page a title, such as “Someday” or “Ideas”. These pages hold lists that outlast any single day.
  5. Fold used pages behind the cover. The spiral lets you keep a single clean page in front of you.

When the front pages and the back pages meet in the middle, the notebook is full.

Put a symbol at the start of every line. It tells you at a glance what still needs you.

One day on paper

Step through a single day. The same page changes from morning to evening, then the unfinished work carries onto the next page.

If a task is carried forward more than three times, change it. Break it into a smaller first step, schedule it as an event, or drop it. A task that never gets done is clutter.

Lists and trackers at the back

Anything you want to outlive a single day goes at the back. Start with two or three and add others only when you notice you need them.

Other lists that work well here: an idea list (copy your ! items here from the day pages), a mood or energy log with one word per day, or books and films to try.

Using the spiral

The binding is what sets this notebook apart from a book. Use it on purpose.

Hand someone a note

Write it on its own page, draw a box round it and tear it out. Write › and where it went on the page it came from, so you know it is not lost.

Keep private things private

Put anything sensitive on a page by itself and tear it out when you are done. Shred it rather than binning it if it matters. Passwords are better kept somewhere else altogether.

Fix a mistake cleanly

Tear the page out and start again. A ruined page in a bound book stays there for ever.

Keep a clean working page

Fold finished pages behind the cover. You always write on a fresh sheet, and the wire does not dig into your hand.

Tear without ripping

Fold the page back flat against the wire and crease it first, then pull. Cheap pages tear unevenly if you pull straight out.

Keep what matters. If a torn-out page holds something worth keeping, photograph it or tape it into a bound notebook before you throw it away.

Weekly review

Ten minutes once a week stops the notebook turning into a pile of old pages. Pick a regular time, such as Sunday evening.

  • Flip through the week’s pages and look for dots with no cross.
  • Carry forward, move (›) or drop each one.
  • Copy any ! ideas worth keeping to the idea list at the back.
  • Total the spend tracker and check the habit boxes.
  • Check “Waiting for” and chase anything overdue.
  • Tear out and bin pages with nothing left on them.

When the notebook is full

Look through it once more. Copy anything still open onto page 2 of the next notebook, keep the lists you still use, and write the end date on the cover. Store finished notebooks in a box for a year, then decide whether to keep or recycle them.

Make your own

The same system works in a stapled notebook you make yourself. The differences are that pages cannot be torn out cleanly and there is no wire to fold behind.

  1. Take 6 sheets of A4 printer paper and cut each in half across the middle. That gives you 12 pieces of A5.
  2. Fold each piece in half. Each folded piece makes 4 pages of about 105 × 148 mm.
  3. Nest the folded pieces inside one another, with a folded cardstock sheet as the cover.
  4. Staple twice through the fold, or sew with a needle and thread through 3 holes. A long-reach stapler makes this much easier.
  5. Round the corners with scissors so it slides in and out of a pocket.

This gives a notebook of 48 pages, about the same as a Field Notes book. Printer paper shows ink through the page, so use a ballpoint, gel pen or pencil.

Without a spiral, adapt the tricks: keep loose notes on a slip of paper tucked in the back, and use a ruler and blade along the fold if you need a clean tear-out.
